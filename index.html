Objectives & Importance

Why is it important in business? In the corporate world, clarity is key. We use the Present Simple to talk about:

Company Facts

What your company does, makes, or provides.

Regular Routines

Daily schedules, weekly meetings, and standard procedures.

Fixed Schedules

Flight times, project deadlines, and public timetables.

Permanent Situations

Office locations and long-term job roles.

Core Sentence Structure

How to build affirmative sentences in a professional context:

Subject  +  Base Verb  (+ add -s / -es for He / She / It)

SubjectExample
I · You · We · They "We operate in over 20 countries."
He · She · It "Our CEO manages the global strategy."
Company / Platform "The platform updates the metrics automatically."

Tip: Always remember the -s or -es for third-person singular — He, She, It, or a singular company name.

Business Uses & Examples

Use Case Explanation Corporate Example
Company Facts Long-term truths & policies. "We deliver solutions to tech firms."
Routines / Habits Repeated corporate activities. "The HR team conducts interviews weekly."
Fixed Schedules Upcoming timetabled events. "The train leaves tonight at 6 PM."
Permanent States Office locations and jobs. "Our main headquarters is in London."

Negative & Interrogative Forms

To handle business meetings and professional correspondence smoothly, use the auxiliaries Do / Does:

Negative

Subject + do / does not (don't / doesn't) + Base Verb

"We do not (don't) offer refunds after 30 days."

"He does not (doesn't) work in the marketing department."

Interrogative

Do / Does + Subject + Base Verb?

"Do you need the financial reports today?"

"Does the client agree to our proposal terms?"

Adverbs of Frequency in Business

We use adverbs to show how often an operation or task happens:

Always "We always verify our data before publishing."
Usually "Our managers usually meet on Monday mornings."
Often "The IT team often updates the security firewalls."
Rarely / Seldom "They rarely change their core pricing models."

Position: these adverbs go before the main verb, but after the verb to be.
